The Story

Welcome to the lobby of the Beta Hall Philharmonic. Every night, an over-eager conductor tries to drive the orchestra faster and harder than it can safely go — exactly what an overstimulated heart does under catecholamine drive. A pair of calm ushers move in: one dims the houselights (blocking the signal), the other holds up a slowing metronome (falling heart rate), while the grandfather clock behind them ticks in slow motion (falling contractility).

Around the symmetric marble staircase, more of the story unfolds: a velvet rope keeps some ushers working only the brass section (cardioselective agents sparing the lungs), while a less careful usher ropes off the woodwinds too (non-selective agents also hitting the airways). A fast-moving usher sprints through everything and is gone in seconds (esmolol's ultra-short action). Two ushers wearing alpha and beta badges gently attend to a wheelchair-bound patron (mixed-antagonists treating heart failure, very carefully). Around the edges of the room: a man warms cold hands by the fire, another slumps fatigued under a blanket, a chalkboard tracks rising lipids next to a slice of cake, a "Survivors" table toasts a second chance after a heart attack, a migraine sufferer wears ear protection, a nervous violinist is calmed before a solo, and an eye doctor administers drops beneath a glowing eye chart.

Legend — 18 Symbols

  1. Leaping conductor: an overstimulated heart driven by catecholamines
  2. Dimmer-switch usher: beta-blockade, the core mechanism
  3. Slowing metronome: ↓ heart rate (negative chronotropy)
  4. Slow-motion pendulum clock: ↓ contractility (negative inotropy)
  5. Roped-off brass section: cardioselective (β1) agents — metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol
  6. Roped-off woodwind section: non-selective agents also block β2 — propranolol, nadolol, timolol
  7. Stop-gesture at the door: contraindicated in asthma/COPD (bronchospasm risk)
  8. Sprinting usher: esmolol, ultra-short-acting, IV rate control
  9. Alpha/beta badge pair: labetalol/carvedilol, mixed alpha+beta antagonists
  10. Wheelchair patron, heart badge: careful titration needed in heart failure
  11. Man slumped under a blanket: fatigue and depression, common side effects
  12. Man warming hands at the fire: peripheral vasoconstriction, cold extremities
  13. Bar chart + slice of cake: can worsen lipid profile
  14. "Survivors" table, raised glasses: post-MI mortality benefit
  15. Person with ear muffs: migraine prophylaxis
  16. Violinist calmed with water: performance/situational anxiety
  17. Eye drops beneath a glowing eye chart: timolol for open-angle glaucoma
